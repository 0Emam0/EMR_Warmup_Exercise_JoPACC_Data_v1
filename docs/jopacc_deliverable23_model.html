Deliverables 2 and 3 · Data dictionary and data model

What can actually be extracted, and the shape to load it in

The JoPACC reports are designed infographics, not data documents. That fact decides the grain, and the grain decides everything else. This describes what the three report types contain, what survives extraction, and the model the pipeline loads into.

01 — Are the three report types the same?

Significantly different in layout, closely shared in vocabulary

This distinction matters, because it means one data model is right but one extraction strategy is not.

The one category that cannot be extracted. The annual reports plot three years of monthly series with an axis and no data labels. Reading them would mean measuring pixel heights against a gridline, which produces numbers that look precise and are not. They are omitted and logged as a known gap rather than estimated. Nothing is lost: the same figures exist at full precision in the monthly reports, which the pipeline does extract.

03 — Grain

One row is one number as printed on one page

The obvious grain would be one row per period per system per metric. That grain is wrong for this source, and the reason is worth stating plainly.

To load a row at that grain you must assert that a given figure is the transaction count for CliQ in August 2026. On a labelled bar chart that assertion is safe. On a KPI card whose only system marker is a logo image, or on a poster panel where a value sits between two captions, it is a guess. A model that demands the assertion forces the pipeline to guess on every page where it cannot know, and those guesses become indistinguishable from the figures it is sure about.

Loading at the printed-value grain keeps the uncertainty visible. Every row carries the page, the panel, the panel title, the label it was matched to and a note where no match was possible. A downstream model can promote these into a period-by-system-by-metric fact table, and can do it under review, with the unmatched rows in plain sight rather than silently resolved.

Keys and relationships

  • Primary key — record_id, unique within a workbook.
  • Candidate key — source_pdf + source_page + x_pt + y_pt. A value's position is stable across re-runs, which makes this the key to use when comparing two extractions of the same file.
  • Foreign keys — source_pdf to the report inventory; source_pdf + section_key to the section map; source_pdf + source_page to the page log.
  • Join key to other JoPACC data — report_type + reporting_period.

Why raw_value is kept next to the normalised number. It costs a column and it is the only way to check a figure without reopening the PDF. 16.64B JOD beside 16640000000 makes a scale error visible at a glance; the normalised number alone does not.

Three traps that produce plausible wrong numbers rather than errors.

A lost decimal point turns 1.74 M into 174M — a hundredfold error that parses cleanly and passes every range check. Only the disagreement between OCR passes exposes it, which is why competing readings are kept rather than discarded.

A stripped percent sign turns 81.3% into a count of 81.3, and a dropped scale suffix turns 1.74 M into 1.74. Both arrive as separate text runs and both look like stray characters.

The footer date stamp 08.2026 parses as the number 8.2026 and would otherwise appear once per page in the fact table.

06 — Reconciliation, and its limits

Two checks are safe on this source and are run:

  • Shares sum to 100% within a panel, where exactly two percentages appear. This is the shape the reports use for money-transfer/purchase and on-us/off-us splits.
  • Values fall inside plausible bounds for their unit.

Totals are deliberately not checked against the sum of their parts. The reports present figures that look additive and are not. The cross-system summary counts users of each system separately, and anyone holding both a bank account on CliQ and a mobile wallet on JoMoPay is counted in both; the totals are not intended to be a sum. Asserting the identity would generate a permanent stream of false failures and train whoever reads the validation sheet to ignore it.

07 — Detecting change

The layout has already changed once inside this two-year window, and will change again. Four signals are recorded rather than smoothed over:

  • An unrecognised section divider is reported as a structural change, not dropped. This is how a new chapter announces itself.
  • A missing expected section is reported the same way. This is how the seven-section 2024 layout distinguishes itself from the eight-section one.
  • A page yielding nothing is logged with its page number, so a redesign that defeats extraction is visible rather than silent.
  • A changed content hash under an unchanged filename forces reprocessing. JoPACC replaces published files without renaming them, and five files in this window have already been replaced this way.
